# Rewilding field notes

A short field guide to habitat recovery. This sample source is included so you can try the workspace before adding your own documents.

## What is passive rewilding?

Passive rewilding gives natural processes room to resume. A land manager might reduce grazing pressure, leave fallen timber in place, or allow a river to move across its floodplain. Active rewilding adds a deliberate intervention, such as planting missing native trees or restoring a blocked watercourse. Both approaches depend on the starting condition of the site.

## How do beavers change rivers?

Beavers build dams from branches, stones and mud. A series of dams can slow water, create shallow ponds and reconnect a stream with its floodplain. The resulting mix of open water, wet meadow and woodland creates different habitats in a small area. Effects vary between sites and should be assessed with local observations.

## How is recovery measured?

Start with a baseline. Record the vegetation, water levels, signs of wildlife and land-use history before changing management. Return to the same monitoring points through the seasons. Pair repeat photographs with a small set of consistent measures, such as native plant cover or the length of stream connected to its floodplain. Compare changes across several years.
